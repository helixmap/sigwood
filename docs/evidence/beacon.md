# beacon calibration record

beacon scores connection flows for periodic timing using an FFT over a binned time
grid. This record covers how its constants got their values, what the reference
figure is anchored on, and where the evidence stops.

## what was measured

Two separate things, and the distinction matters:

- **The scorer's behavior**, measured against seeded synthetic connection trains. The
  score is a function of timing alone, so a synthetic train exercises it completely.
  No real capture is required to know what the scorer does with a given cadence.
- **The detector's severity ceiling and its finding copy**, settled by a design review
  that verified every load-bearing claim against the source before acting on it.

What was *not* measured is a precision or recall rate. No such figure exists for
beacon, and none is published.

## data

- A seeded synthetic corpus (the shipped demo generator) carrying a known 180-second
  beacon: 480 connections over 24 hours.
- Seeded synthetic trains at a range of cadences, used to establish the grid's
  representable period floor and the anchor-sensitivity of cadences that land exactly
  on it.
- One real home network's connection logs, as the deployment where the detector's
  output volume was observed. This is a single environment. It is the reason no
  precision claim ships.

## the gates applied

The reference anchor had to be **reproducible**: seeded, generated by shipped code,
and pinned in tests through the live generator import path rather than a copied
number. A free-shape or band-edge cadence was rejected as an anchor. Cadences that
are an exact multiple of the bin size are phase-sensitive, scoring anywhere across a
wide band depending on where arrivals fall relative to bin boundaries, so they cannot
anchor a public figure.

Copy had to be **reproducible or removed**. A precision figure that had shipped in a
source comment since the first release could not be traced to any measurement, so it
was removed rather than restated; the module's qualitative wording is what remains.

## outcome

The seeded 180-second flow scores **~0.62** with a dominant period of exactly
180.0 seconds. That is a single-day, favorable-realization figure, and it is labeled
as such. Resolving a jittered periodic beacon reliably takes about a week of span; at
a single day the same cadence clears the threshold only intermittently.

The unmeasured precision claim was removed. Severity was capped: the composite is
timing evidence from one category, and the tier that asserts more than that requires
corroboration from an independent category the detector cannot supply on its own.

## what is frozen as a result

- **30-second bins.** The bin size sets the FFT's period floor at twice the bin, fixes
  which cadences alias, and every other number is tuned against it. Changing it means
  re-deriving the threshold, the period band, the prominence normalization, and the
  reference figure together.
- **The period band.** The floor sits deliberately below the grid's own representable
  minimum, as inert margin that stays correct if the bin size ever shrinks. The ceiling
  is two hours.
- **The composite weighting:** 40% spectral ratio, 40% peak prominence, 20% inverted
  jitter, with prominence normalized at 100x.
- **A minimum of 20 connections** per candidate flow, above the scorer's own hard
  sample floor.
- **A dense-allocation ceiling** on the number of scorable bins, checked before any
  bin-proportional allocation. A flow whose timestamps span an implausible range, say
  ten lines dated years apart, would otherwise request billions of bins. It abstains
  rather than coarsening the bin size to fit, because silently re-binning would change
  scores to dodge a memory bound.
- **Severity caps at MEDIUM.** The higher tier is structurally unreachable from timing
  evidence alone.

## limitations

- **A sub-60-second cadence is misreported, not missed.** It aliases to a longer
  period; the reported figure is wrong in a specific, documented direction. Only finer
  bins would fix truthful sub-60 reporting.
- **A cadence sitting exactly on the floor scores by luck.** Identical 60-second
  beacons can score across a wide band depending on jitter realization.
- **The evidence is one home network, synthetics, and published method literature.**
  There is no measured precision, no labeled-dataset validation, and no second
  deployment. Widening that is gated on real data from an additional benign
  deployment plus controlled callback evidence, neither of which sigwood has.
- **The scored population is narrower than "connections."** Only completed connections
  carrying observed originator bytes are scored, and the byte value itself never
  enters the score.
- **Rotation and cadence changes defeat it.** A flow that moves between destinations,
  or changes its interval mid-window, does not present as one periodic series.

## reproducibility

The single-estate corpus carries real network data and is not published. The seeded
generator, pinned reference score and dominant period, and scorer tests that fix the
period-band boundaries are reproducible from the repository. Those anchors, rather than
the private corpus, support the claims in this record.
